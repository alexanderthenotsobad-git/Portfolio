02 / About

I like the messy middle — where data, design, and real people meet.

A decade in data and product engineering, one throughline: make complex systems easier to use.

My first programming project was a flight simulator in BASIC. I wanted to rebuild every part of flight — a very roundabout way of saying I've always been more interested in how the whole system works than in one shiny piece of it.

That instinct took me through a decade of data and product work: Excel models to MS SQL, an Oracle data lake, Spotfire dashboards that became flagship products, and a $3.5B TV ratings platform at Nielsen. Along the way I learned that the hardest part of software is rarely the code — it's making the system legible to the people using it.

One chapter still means the most. Not long after 9/11, while I was in civil service, my agency — the New York City Department of Design and Construction — was charged with restoring the site at Ground Zero. I worked long, extraordinary days alongside colleagues who showed up for the same reason I did, mostly without extra pay, out of loyalty to the mission and a plain, deep dedication to the city we served. It was the most fulfilling work I've ever done, and it's where I learned that the steady, committed effort — the show-up-every-day kind — is what moves things forward.

Now I'm building VRS, an AI-assisted restaurant platform, and teaching enterprise engineering at the University of Florida. That same wholehearted sense of purpose still drives me: I care about clear schemas, calm interfaces, and shipping the unglamorous thing that makes someone's day shorter.
